C++ Grammar Notes from the Workbench

A living notebook of rules, nuances, and curiosities from modern C++.

May 18, 2026C++5 min read

C++ grammar rewards careful reading. The language often says exactly what it means, but it says it in a compact dialect of declarations, value categories, and lookup rules.

This workbench note gathers the small rules that make larger code easier to reason about.

Declarations carry more shape than they first show

A declaration is not just a name and a type. It can encode storage duration, linkage, cv-qualification, reference binding, and the narrow route through overload resolution.

When a line feels surprising, redraw it as a relationship between a name, a type, and an initialization form.

In C++, punctuation is often architecture wearing a very small coat.

Small syntax choices change ownership

Copies, moves, references, and views can look visually similar. Naming the lifetime expectation next to the syntax makes review safer.
